==🟠**Compound Condition**==

- *Combining multiple conditions using `AND` and `OR` operators is called a compound condition.*
- *`AND` returns `TRUE` only when all conditions are true.*
- *`OR` returns `TRUE` when at least one condition is true.*

==_Truth Table for_ `AND`==

| Condition 1 | Condition 2 | Result |
| ----------- | ----------- | ------ |
| TRUE        | TRUE        | TRUE   |
| TRUE        | FALSE       | FALSE  |
| FALSE       | TRUE        | FALSE  |
| FALSE       | FALSE       | FALSE  |

==_Truth Table for_ `OR`==

| Condition 1 | Condition 2 | Result |
| ----------- | ----------- | ------ |
| TRUE        | TRUE        | TRUE   |
| TRUE        | FALSE       | TRUE   |
| FALSE       | TRUE        | TRUE   |
| FALSE       | FALSE       | FALSE  |

==🔵***Examples of Compound Conditions***==

*1. Employees earning more than 40000 or working in department 10*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE salary > 40000 OR dept = 10;
```

*2. Employees whose names are Arman Khan, Priya Sharma, or Vijay Sen*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE name = 'Arman Khan'
   OR name = 'Priya Sharma'
   OR name = 'Vijay Sen';
```

*3. Employees working in department 10 and earning more than 30000*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE dept = 10 AND salary > 30000;
```

*4. Employees earning more than 30000 and less than 50000*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE salary > 30000 AND salary < 50000;
```

*5. Employees who joined in the year 2020*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE hiredate >= DATE '2020-01-01'
  AND hiredate < DATE '2021-01-01';
```

*6. Employees working in department 10 or department 30*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE dept = 10 OR dept = 30;
```

*7. Employees in department 10 earning more than 40000 and receiving commission*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE dept = 10
  AND salary > 40000
  AND comm IS NOT NULL;
```

**_Operator Precedence (_**`**_AND_**` **_and_** `**_OR_**`**_)_**

- In Oracle, `AND` has higher precedence than `OR`.
- Therefore, `AND` is evaluated before `OR` when parentheses are not used.

*Example 1: Employees earning more than 40000 or working in department 20 with salary greater than 30000*

Without parentheses:

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE salary > 40000 OR dept = 20 AND salary > 30000;
```

Oracle evaluates the condition as:

```sql
WHERE salary > 40000
   OR (dept = 20 AND salary > 30000);
```

This displays all employees earning more than 40000, regardless of department, and employees in department 20 earning more than 30000.

To display employees earning more than 30000 from either department 20 or department 30, use parentheses:

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE (dept = 20 OR dept = 30) AND salary > 30000;
```

*Example 2: Employees in Hyderabad or Delhi whose salary is greater than 30000*

==🔴Without parentheses:==

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE city = 'Delhi' OR city = 'Hyderabad' AND salary > 30000;
```

This condition is evaluated as:

```sql
WHERE city = 'Delhi'
   OR (city = 'Hyderabad' AND salary > 30000);
```

This displays all employees from Delhi, regardless of salary, and employees from Hyderabad earning more than 30000.

To apply the salary condition to both cities, use parentheses:

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE (city = 'Delhi' OR city = 'Hyderabad')
  AND salary > 30000;
```

**_Additional Compound Condition Queries_**

*8. Employees whose salary is between 30000 and 50000 and belong to department 10 or 20*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE salary >= 30000
  AND salary <= 50000
  AND (dept = 10 OR dept = 20);
```

*9. Employees who joined after 2022 or receive commission greater than 250*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE hiredate > DATE '2022-12-31'
   OR comm > 250;
```

*10. Employees who belong to department 30 and have either NULL commission or salary greater than 40000*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE dept = 30
  AND (comm IS NULL OR salary > 40000);
```

*11. Employees who do not belong to department 10 or 20*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE dept <> 10
  AND dept <> 20;
```

*12. Employees who joined between 2020 and 2023 and earn more than 30000*

```sql
SELECT *
FROM emp
WHERE hiredate >= DATE '2020-01-01'
  AND hiredate < DATE '2024-01-01'
  AND salary > 30000;
```

==🔴**_Important Notes_**==

- Use `AND` when all conditions must be satisfied.
- Use `OR` when at least one condition must be satisfied.
- Use parentheses `()` to control the order of condition evaluation.
- `AND` has higher precedence than `OR`.
- Use `IS NULL` and `IS NOT NULL` to check NULL values. Do not use `= NULL` or `<> NULL`.
- The `EMP` table does not contain `JOB`, `AGE`, or text-based department names, so those questions cannot be used with this table.
